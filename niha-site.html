About

Practitioner. Analyst. Reflective thinker.

I am a Qualified Behaviour Analyst with an MSc in Educational and Developmental Psychology from University College London, practising in Dubai, UAE. My work is grounded in the evidence base of Applied Behaviour Analysis and guided by a deep commitment to the autonomy, dignity, and lived experience of every client I work with.

I believe ABA at its best makes people's worlds bigger — not more manageable. That means skills-affirming practice, assent-based approaches, and goals shaped by what a good life looks like for that person, not what is easiest to measure.

"Our job is not to make someone easier to manage. Our job is to make their world bigger."

Approach

How I practise

Good ABA isn't a single technique. It's a set of values that shape every decision — from how goals are written to how a session begins.

01

Assent-based

Every client has a voice. I actively monitor for signs of assent and withdrawal — and I respond to both with equal care.

02

Skills-affirming

Goals are built around what gives a person access to a better life — not around making them appear more typical.

03

Evidence-based

Practice decisions are grounded in peer-reviewed research and continuously evaluated against real-world data.

04

Person-centred

A good life looks different for every person. I ask what matters to the individual before I ask what to target.

05

Family-inclusive

Families are essential partners, not passive recipients. Progress built together is progress that lasts.

06

Culturally responsive

The UAE is one of the world's most diverse communities. My practice is shaped by that reality — not despite it.

Thoughts

From the field

Reflections on ABA practice, ethics, and the evolution of the field — shared on Instagram and written here for the wider community.

Reflective Practice

ABA in 2000 vs ABA now — the field changed. Did your practice?

Discrete trial. Dense demand. Compliance first. Extinction as standard. The goal was behaviour change — and the measure of success was whether the child did what they were told. We didn't talk about assent. We didn't talk about autonomy.

Autonomy & Choice

ABA without autonomy isn't good ABA

ABA at its best isn't about shaping behaviour for the convenience of others. It's about giving people the tools to live their lives, on their terms. Choice is the everyday practice. Autonomy is the bigger vision.

Assent

What assent actually looks like in a session

Assent isn't the absence of protest. It's the presence of an active, observable indicator of willingness — and it should be operationally defined, individualised, and monitored throughout every session.
